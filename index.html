Active Street Flood Early Warning & Drainage Monitoring

NYC FloodGuard upgrades passive flood reporting into an active early-warning prototype. Paired with an Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi, HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, float switches, and a 3D-printed NYC street model, it tracks water depth in centimeters, measures how fast water is rising or draining, and estimates time until configurable danger thresholds.

Hardware & Engineering Architecture

About NYC FloodGuard

How the physical tabletop prototype works, why it improves on passive flood logging, and how to safely connect your Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi.

What Makes NYC FloodGuard Different

NYC FloodNet provides invaluable historical street flood observations across New York City. NYC FloodGuard builds on that inspiration by focusing on active early warning and drainage feedback:

  • Rising-Water Velocity: Computes smoothed rate of change (cm/min) over a rolling window instead of looking only at static depth.
  • Conservative Early-Warning ETA: Projects estimated time to Warning and Critical thresholds when water is rising reliably, and honestly shows Not available when water is stable, draining, or noisy.
  • Drainage Monitoring: Explicitly detects falling water levels when the drainage pump operates so observers can verify that conditions are improving.
  • Side-by-Side Real-World Context: Keeps prototype measurements clearly separated from authentic historical NYC FloodNet records.

How Flood Detection & Early Warning Work

Water Depth (cm) = Sensor Mounting Height (cm) − Measured Distance to Water (cm)
  • Step 1 — Calibrated Ultrasonic Depth: The HC-SR04 measures air distance to the water surface in centimeters. Subtracting from the dry mounting height yields water depth.
  • Step 2 — Splash & Ripple Filtering: On-board 5-ping median filtering plus a browser rolling median window suppress ripples and sudden single-frame echo spikes.
  • Step 3 — Trend & Threshold Evaluation: Linear regression computes slope (cm/min) and goodness-of-fit (R²). Status maps clearly to NORMAL (Green), WARNING (Orange), and CRITICAL (Red).
  • Step 4 — Mathematical Projection: When rising reliably, ETA = (Threshold − Depth) / Rise Rate. This is a mathematical projection, not a guaranteed weather forecast.

Physical Prototype Hardware Components

Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi

Dual-core STM32H747XI microcontroller (3.3V GPIO logic) reading ultrasonic pulses, float switches, and streaming JSON over USB Serial at 115200 baud.

Arduino GIGA Display Shield

Local touchscreen display module planned for on-model water depth and warning status readout alongside the web dashboard.

HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensor

Mounted above the 3D-printed street surface to measure air distance to water continuously without contacting floodwater.

Float Switches

Physical backup water-level switches (Low & High) wired with INPUT_PULLUP to verify water contact independently of ultrasonic echoes.

Two Water Pumps (Manual Demo)

One inflow pump to simulate rising street flooding and one drainage pump to demonstrate falling water recovery. Operated manually until relay hardware is added.

3D-Printed NYC Street Model & Optional Barrier

Scale NYC street and building basin for controlled testing, with optional future support for a servo-controlled flood barrier.

3.3V GPIO Safety & Sensor Calibration

Critical 3.3V Warning (Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi): GIGA R1 GPIO pins are 3.3V only and are NOT 5V tolerant. A standard 5V HC-SR04 outputs 5V on its ECHO pin — always connect ECHO through a 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ voltage divider (steps 5V down to 3.33V) or a 5V-to-3.3V logic level shifter before wiring to pin D9.
  • Wiring: VCC → 5V, GND → GND, TRIG → D8, ECHO → 1kΩ/2kΩ divider → D9, Float switches → D6 / D7 to GND.
  • Calibration: Empty the 3D-printed street model completely dry, measure the distance from the HC-SR04 sensor face to the street floor (in cm), and set MOUNT_HEIGHT_CM in both the sketch and Administrator Settings.
  • Browser Requirement: Web Serial requires desktop Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge over HTTPS or localhost.

USB Serial JSON Format (115200 baud)

The firmware emits one newline-terminated JSON object per second (arduino/nyc_floodguard_giga.ino): 

{
  "device": "GIGA-R1-WIFI",
  "mount_height_cm": 25.00,
  "distance_cm": 18.40,
  "water_depth_cm": 6.60,
  "float_low": true,
  "float_high": false,
  "barrier_state": "not_installed",
  "uptime_ms": 14200
}

Project Status: Completed vs. Planned Features

Completed & Working

  • Supabase email authentication (register, sign in, logout, password reset, persistent sessions).
  • Real Web Serial USB connection for Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi with backup simulation mode.
  • Centimeter water-depth math, median ripple filtering, rising/falling drainage trend, and conservative threshold ETA.
  • Official NYC FloodNet historical API search, filtering, pagination (>1,000 rows), and per-user Supabase bookmarks.
  • SQL migration prepared for database-enforced Administrator / Regular User roles and RLS-protected threshold settings.

Planned / Requires Hardware or Setup

  • Wi-Fi Cloud Sync (Unfinished): Direct automatic upload from Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi to Supabase tables over Wi-Fi.
  • Automatic Pump Control (Planned): Relay driver circuit & safety interlock firmware (pumps are currently manual).
  • Servo Flood Barrier (Optional Future): Firmware hook included (ENABLE_SERVO_BARRIER), not required for primary demo.
  • GIGA Display Shield UI (Planned): On-device local readout screen mirroring serial depth & warnings.
