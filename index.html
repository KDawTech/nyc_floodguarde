    1. Why New York City Floods

    Understanding the physical drivers of urban and coastal flooding across NYC’s 520 miles of coastline and 7,500-mile sewer network.

    Research-Backed Context
    Core Physical Principle: Flooding occurs whenever water enters an area faster than it can drain, infiltrate, or escape. In a dense coastal city like New York, that imbalance can come from extreme rainfall from above, storm surge from the harbor, or infrastructure failure underground.
    CAUSE 01

    Heavy Rainfall Exceeding Drainage Capacity

    NYC’s combined and separate sewer pipes are engineered for routine storms. Sudden, intense “cloudburst” downpours deliver rainwater faster than underground pipes can convey it, causing rapid street and basement flooding.

    CAUSE 02

    Blocked Storm Drains & Debris

    NYC DEP maintains over 150,000 street catch basins. When leaves, trash, or sediment mat over a catch-basin grate, surface runoff cannot enter the sewer, creating deep local ponding even during moderate rain.

    CAUSE 03

    Low-Lying Neighborhoods & Impervious Pavement

    Asphalt streets, concrete sidewalks, and dense rooftops block natural soil infiltration. Runoff concentrates rapidly in low-lying neighborhoods, underpasses, and below-grade cellar entrances.

    CAUSE 04

    Coastal Storm Surge & High Tides

    Onshore winds during tropical or coastal storms push ocean and harbor water inland, especially at high tide. Elevated harbor levels also block sewer outfalls from draining street runoff.

    CAUSE 05

    Climate-Related Increases in Flood Risk

    A warmer atmosphere holds more moisture, increasing the frequency of extreme short-duration cloudburst downpours, while long-term sea-level rise raises baseline high tides and storm-surge reach (alongside localized water-main or sewer failures).

    Coastal Surge Example: Hurricane Sandy (October 2012)

    Hurricane Sandy illustrates coastal flood risk. Its massive wind field coincided with peak high tide, driving a historic saltwater storm surge that inundated roughly 50 square miles (~17% of NYC’s land mass), flooded subway tunnels and power substations, and exposed the vulnerability of NYC’s low-lying shoreline.

    What Our Tabletop Model Demonstrates: Blocked-Drain Flooding

    NYC FloodGuard’s 3D-printed street, catch basin, circulation pump, and removable blockage demonstrate rainfall-related blocked-drain (pluvial) flooding. It isolates how quickly street water climbs from dry road into WARNING and CRITICAL depths when storm-drain outflow is obstructed — and how restoring drain flow allows water to recede.

    2. NYC FloodGuard 2.0 vs. NYC FloodNet

    How our student prototype builds on the inspiration of NYC FloodNet without overstating accuracy or operational readiness.

    Honest Comparison

    What NYC FloodNet Provides (Operational City Network)

    • Citywide Deployment: Real, weatherproof ultrasonic flood sensors installed across all five NYC boroughs by NYU, CUNY, NYCEM, and NYC Mayor’s Office of Climate & Environmental Justice.
    • Public Historical Record: Publishes authentic, long-term street flood event records directly to NYC Open Data (integrated separately in our NYC FloodNet Data tab).
    • Agency Situational Awareness: Feeds verified street-level inundation data into city emergency planning, research, and post-storm analysis.

    What NYC FloodGuard 2.0 Demonstrates (Student Prototype)

    • Local Active Early-Warning Concept: Computes rolling-window water rise rate (cm/min), trend direction, and short-term threshold ETAs when measurements are reliable.
    • Weather + Rule-Based Resident Guidance: Pairs local tabletop readings with live Open-Meteo NYC rainfall forecasts and status-based protective actions (NORMAL / WARNING / CRITICAL).
    • Interactive Blocked-Drain Education: Demonstrates how a clogged catch basin causes rapid ponding and how clearing debris restores drainage in real time.
    Scope Honesty: NYC FloodGuard 2.0 is a tabletop classroom engineering prototype. It does not claim superior measurement accuracy, official municipal deployment, or scientifically validated citywide flood-prediction performance compared to NYC FloodNet.

    3. Sensor Accuracy, Surface Splashing & Physical Limitations

    Engineering response to: “What happens when water splashes, waves move, or the sensor shakes?”

    Honest Error Boundaries
    Important Engineering Honesty: The HC-SR04 is an educational, low-cost ultrasonic distance sensor — it is not perfectly accurate and is sensitive to wave turbulence, vibration, and acoustic reflections. NYC FloodGuard combines mechanical stabilization with multi-stage filtering and explicit uncertainty reporting rather than pretending every echo is exact.

    Seven-Sample Median Filtering

    Each Arduino measurement cycle collects a burst of 7 ultrasonic pings, sorts them, and takes the middle (median) value. A single splash spike or dropped echo falls at the extreme high or low end of the burst and is rejected before depth is reported.

    Three-Reading Alert Confirmation

    To prevent a momentary ripple or passing wave crest from triggering a false alarm, the firmware requires 3 consecutive confirmed readings above the WARNING (0.50 cm) or CRITICAL (1.25 cm) threshold before escalating the official alert state.

    Locked Dry-Road Calibration

    Depth is computed from a calibrated dry street reference (Depth = Dry Baseline − Distance). The dashboard locks that dry-road baseline so rising water never silently shifts zero; recalibration (serial command c) requires explicit user confirmation that the street is dry.

    Sensor Mounting & Vibration

    Because even a 3–5 mm shift in riser height directly biases water depth, the ultrasonic mount must be rigidly secured to the street basin. Mechanical shaking introduces distance jitter that degrades trend confidence and triggers Unstable sensor quality warnings.

    Ultrasonic Reflections & False Readings

    Steep waves, pump turbulence, foam, or nearby 3D-printed building walls can scatter or reflect the 40 kHz sound cone, creating multipath spikes or missed echoes. Readings inside the ~2 cm blind zone or outside basin geometry are flagged Out of Range or Suspicious — never silently treated as 0.00 cm dry road.

    Independent Validation Is Required

    A sensor cannot prove its own accuracy. Quantifying true bias and uncertainty (e.g., ±mm error across calm vs. splashing water) requires comparing ultrasonic outputs against an independent physical reference such as a calibrated millimeter ruler, graduated gauge, or contact level sensor across repeated trials.

    4. Early-Warning Capability: Three Distinct Layers

    Separating current flood detection, rising-water rate tracking, and pre-flood rainfall forecasting — without overclaiming exact arrival times.

    Scope Transparency
    LAYER 1 Physically Tested

    Detecting Current Flooding

    Measures standing water depth on the 3D-printed street model via Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi + HC-SR04 and confirms NORMAL (<0.50 cm), WARNING (0.50–1.24 cm), or CRITICAL (≥1.25 cm) status using 7-sample median filtering and 3-reading confirmation. Verified physically up to 2.42 cm CRITICAL.

    LAYER 2 Software-Tested / Experimental

    Detecting Rapidly Rising Water

    Evaluates rate of change (cm/min) over a rolling time window using linear regression and statistical quality gating. Reports Rising Water, rapid-rise alerts, or suspected blocked drainage only when measurements are consistent; reports Uncertain when noisy.

    LAYER 3 Software-Tested Context

    Forecasting Rainfall Risk Before Flooding

    Fetches live 12-hour NYC precipitation forecasts from Open-Meteo to highlight upcoming moderate or heavy rainfall that could stress urban drains before street ponding begins. Clearly labeled as external weather context, separate from observed sensor depth.

    Why We Do Not Claim Exact Flood-Time Predictions: When sensor quality is Good and water rises steadily on the tabletop model, the dashboard computes a simple mathematical projection (Remaining Depth ÷ Current Rise Rate). This is a short-term linear indicator, not a guaranteed flood clock. Real-world urban flooding depends on nonlinear rainfall bursts, sewer surcharging, street grading, and sudden debris blockages — predicting an exact flood arrival time requires extensive real-world field data and calibrated hydrologic models.

    5. Professor’s Questions Answered

    Concise, honest technical answers prepared for faculty review and live demonstration Q&A. Click any card to collapse or expand.

    10 Core Questions
    Q1What creates urban flooding?

    Urban flooding happens when water enters streets faster than it can drain or infiltrate. In NYC, this is driven by heavy cloudburst rainfall overwhelming sewers, impermeable asphalt and concrete surfaces, debris-blocked catch basins, coastal storm surges/high tides, or water-main and sewer breaks.

    Q2Why did Hurricane Sandy cause major flooding?

    In October 2012, Hurricane Sandy’s exceptionally wide wind field arrived at peak astronomical high tide, pushing a historic coastal storm surge into New York Harbor and the East and Hudson Rivers. Water overtopped shoreline barriers and inundated roughly 17% of NYC’s land area, subway tunnels, and coastal neighborhoods.

    Q3Why is the storm drain important?

    NYC’s ~150,000 catch basins (storm drains) are the primary entry points moving street rainwater into the 7,500-mile underground sewer network. When grates are clear and pipes have capacity, they keep intersections, crosswalks, and building entrances from pooling during everyday storms.

    Q4What happens when the drain becomes blocked?

    Leaves, litter, and sediment matted over a catch basin block outflow while runoff keeps arriving, causing water to pool rapidly right at the curb and spread into intersections and basement entrances. Our 3D-printed street model reproduces this directly: inserting the removable blockage turns steady pump circulation into WARNING and CRITICAL ponding within seconds.

    Q5Why use an ultrasonic sensor?

    The HC-SR04 measures air distance down to the water surface using 40 kHz sound pulses without touching dirty, debris-filled floodwater. It is inexpensive, easy to mount above a street model, and follows the same non-contact overhead sensing principle used in real deployments like NYC FloodNet.

    Q6What happens if the water shakes or splashes?

    Splashes and surface waves scatter ultrasonic echoes and introduce noisy spikes or dropouts. We reduce false alarms using 7-sample median filtering, 3-reading alert confirmation, locked dry calibration, and statistical trend gating (marking noisy trends as Uncertain or flagging suspicious spikes) — while honestly recognizing that severe continuous turbulence still degrades accuracy.

    Q7How accurate is the system?

    It is an educational tabletop prototype, not a certified hydrologic gauge. On a rigidly mounted, dry-calibrated model with calm water it reliably separates dry road from WARNING (≥0.50 cm) and CRITICAL (≥1.25 cm) pooling, but true error bounds (±mm under ripples and vibration) must be measured against an independent ruler or calibrated reference across repeated trials.

    Q8Can the system determine the exact cause of flooding?

    No. A single overhead water-depth sensor only measures surface height over time — it cannot directly see whether water came from a clogged grate, a surcharged sewer, a broken water main, or coastal surge. Combining depth rise with live NYC rainfall data provides helpful clues (such as flagging a suspected blocked drain), but confirming the exact physical cause still requires inspection or additional sensors.

    Q9How does NYC FloodGuard differ from FloodNet?

    NYC FloodNet is an operational citywide network (NYU/CUNY/NYC) measuring real neighborhood street floods across the five boroughs. NYC FloodGuard is a student prototype inspired by FloodNet that focuses on active local early warning — combining rate-of-rise detection, blocked-drain heuristics, live Open-Meteo rainfall context, and an interactive tabletop demonstration — while keeping authentic historical FloodNet Open Data cleanly separated for comparison.

    Q10How could this technology help protect buildings and communities?

    If rebuilt with weatherproof, field-validated sensors near vulnerable basement apartments, subway stairs, underpasses, and ground-floor entrances, localized rising-water alerts could give residents, building superintendents, and maintenance crews crucial extra minutes to clear grates, deploy flood barriers, move vehicles, or avoid entering dangerous standing water.

    6. Possible Real-World Improvements

    Engineering next steps required to move beyond a tabletop classroom model toward real urban deployment.

    Proposed / Future Only

    Local Warnings Near Vulnerable Entrances

    Deploy weatherproof audible/visual warning beacons and neighborhood notifications directly beside basement apartment entries, underground garages, and low-lying lobbies.

    Additional Backup Sensors

    Pair ultrasonic sensors with industrial waterproof transducers, contact float switches, or submerged pressure sensors so two independent physical measurements must agree before triggering critical actions.

    Weather-Informed Early Alerts

    Combine local street depth sensors with official National Weather Service Flash Flood Warnings, Doppler radar rainfall rates, and NOAA harbor tide/surge gauges for multi-hazard context.

    More Reliable Trend Detection

    Upgrade single-sensor regression with multi-sensor fusion, adaptive Kalman filtering, and storm-calibrated anomaly detection that distinguishes vehicles, pedestrians, and splash waves from true rising water.

    Automatic Protective Responses

    Evaluate relay-controlled sump pumps or motorized flood barriers backed by redundant sensors, hardware safety interlocks, and manual overrides so a sensor glitch can never trigger an unsafe mechanical action.

    Testing with Real-World Sensor Data

    Conduct independent ruler/gauge accuracy benchmarks, outdoor weatherproofing trials, and back-testing against multi-year NYC FloodNet storm hydrographs before making operational reliability claims.

    7. Physical Prototype Hardware Components

    Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi

    Dual-core STM32H747XI microcontroller (3.3V GPIO logic) running 7-sample median filtering, 3-reading alert confirmation, and USB Serial streaming at 115200 baud.

    HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensor

    Mounted on a secured riser above the 3D-printed street surface to measure air distance to water continuously without contacting floodwater.

    3D-Printed NYC Street & Storm Drain

    Scale NYC roadway, buildings, and catch-basin drain with a removable drain blockage to physically reproduce clogged-drain urban flooding.

    Two 5V Water Pumps (Manual Control)

    Circulation and drainage pumps used during physical demonstrations to produce rising water when the drain is blocked and receding water when cleared.

    Arduino GIGA Display Shield

    Touchscreen display firmware support for local on-model readout of water depth, alert status, trend, and sensor quality alongside the web dashboard.

    Float Switches & Optional Barrier

    Firmware pins reserved for Low/High contact float switches (D6/D7) and optional future servo barrier (D5, disabled by default).

    3.3V GPIO Safety & Sensor Calibration

    Critical 3.3V Warning (Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi): GIGA R1 GPIO pins are 3.3V only and are NOT 5V tolerant. Connect HC-SR04 ECHO through two 470 Ω resistors in series (940 Ω) toward D10 with one 1 kΩ resistor from D10 to GND (steps 5V safely down to ~2.58V).
    • Wiring: VCC → 5V, GND → GND, TRIG → D9, ECHO → 470Ω + 470Ω → D10 → 1kΩ → GND, Float switches → D6 / D7 to GND.
    • Dry-Road Calibration: Drain the street model completely dry before locking the dry baseline, or use the confirmed manual recalibration control (serial command c).
    • Browser Requirement: Web Serial requires desktop Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge over HTTPS or localhost.
