Active Street Flood Early Warning & Drainage Monitoring

NYC FloodGuard upgrades passive flood reporting into an active early-warning prototype. Paired with an Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi, HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor, float switches, and a 3D-printed NYC street model, it tracks water depth in centimeters, measures how fast water is rising or draining, and estimates time until configurable danger thresholds.

Hardware & Engineering Architecture

About NYC FloodGuard

How the physical tabletop prototype works, why it improves on passive flood logging, and how to safely connect your Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi.

What Makes NYC FloodGuard Different

NYC FloodNet provides invaluable historical street flood observations across New York City. NYC FloodGuard builds on that inspiration by focusing on active early warning and drainage feedback:

  • Rising-Water Velocity: Computes statistically validated rate of change (cm/min) over a rolling window instead of reacting to isolated ripples.
  • Conservative Early-Warning ETA: Projects estimated time to Warning and Critical thresholds only when sensor quality is Good and water rises reliably; otherwise displays Not available.
  • Falling-Water Tracking: Detects statistically reliable Falling Water trends honestly without assuming drainage-pump operation before pump telemetry exists.
  • Side-by-Side Real-World Context: Keeps prototype measurements clearly separated from authentic historical NYC FloodNet records.

How Flood Detection & Early Warning Work

Filtered Water Depth (cm) = Sensor Mounting Height (cm) − Filtered Distance (cm)
  • Step 1 — Physical Range Validation: Distances below 2.0 cm (HC-SR04 blind zone) or beyond mounting height + dry tolerance are flagged Out of Range and never turned into 0.0 cm depth.
  • Step 2 — Spike Rejection & Causal Median: On-board 5-ping median bursts plus browser-side isolated spike rejection and causal trailing median filtering suppress single-frame echo glitches.
  • Step 3 — Statistical Trend & Quality Gating: Linear regression evaluates slope (cm/min), R², and t-statistic so only consistent trends report Rising Water or Falling Water.
  • Step 4 — Mathematical Projection: When sensor quality is Good and rising reliably, ETA = (Threshold − Depth) / Rise Rate. This is a mathematical projection, not a guaranteed weather forecast.

Physical Prototype Hardware Components

Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi

Dual-core STM32H747XI microcontroller (3.3V GPIO logic) reading ultrasonic pulses, float switches, and streaming JSON over USB Serial at 115200 baud.

Arduino GIGA Display Shield

Local touchscreen display module planned for on-model water depth and warning status readout alongside the web dashboard.

HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensor

Mounted above the 3D-printed street surface to measure air distance to water continuously without contacting floodwater.

Float Switches

Physical backup water-level switches (Low & High) wired with INPUT_PULLUP to verify water contact independently of ultrasonic echoes.

Two Water Pumps (Manual Demo)

One inflow pump to simulate rising street flooding and one drainage pump to demonstrate falling water recovery. Operated manually until relay hardware is added.

3D-Printed NYC Street Model & Optional Barrier

Scale NYC street and building basin for controlled testing, with optional future support for a servo-controlled flood barrier.

3.3V GPIO Safety & Sensor Calibration

Critical 3.3V Warning (Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi): GIGA R1 GPIO pins are 3.3V only and are NOT 5V tolerant. A standard 5V HC-SR04 outputs 5V on its ECHO pin — connect ECHO through two 470 Ω resistors in series (940 Ω) toward D10 with one 1 kΩ resistor from D10 to GND (steps 5V safely down to ~2.58V for 3.3V GPIO).
  • Wiring: VCC → 5V, GND → GND, TRIG → D9, ECHO → 470Ω + 470Ω → D10 → 1kΩ → GND, Float switches → D6 / D7 to GND.
  • Calibration: Empty the 3D-printed street model completely dry, measure the distance from the HC-SR04 sensor face to the street floor (in cm), and set MOUNT_HEIGHT_CM in both the sketch and Administrator Settings.
  • Browser Requirement: Web Serial requires desktop Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge over HTTPS or localhost.

Supported USB Serial Formats (115200 baud)

NYC FloodGuard 2.0 automatically parses BOTH the tested human-readable Arduino output and newline-delimited JSON: 

Distance: 14.39 cm | Water Depth: 0.00 cm | Status: NORMAL
Distance: 13.36 cm | Water Depth: 1.03 cm | Status: WARNING
Distance: 12.67 cm | Water Depth: 1.72 cm | Status: CRITICAL

Or structured JSON (arduino/nyc_floodguard_giga.ino):

{"device":"GIGA-R1-WIFI","mount_height_cm":14.39,"distance_cm":13.36,"water_depth_cm":1.03,"quality":"good"}

Project Status: Completed vs. Planned Features

Completed & Working

  • Web Serial USB live connection for Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi (supports both tested pipe-delimited text output and JSON at 115200 baud).
  • Real-time water depth, NORMAL (green) / WARNING (amber) / CRITICAL (red) status, live chart, alert banners, and flood-alert event history.
  • Live NYC weather & 12-hour rainfall forecast via Open-Meteo API with severe rainfall indicators.
  • Rule-based FloodGuard Risk Intelligence with rapid-rise & blocked storm-drain diagnostics.
  • Optional server-side Gemini AI FloodGuard Assistant (when running with Node/Express backend and server-side GEMINI_API_KEY).
  • 6-stage classroom Demo Mode, Supabase Auth + RLS Admin Settings, and NYC FloodNet historical archive + bookmarks.

Requires Configuration / Planned

  • AI Assistant on Static Hosting: Requires the Node/Express server runtime + GEMINI_API_KEY (static GitHub Pages disables AI calls honestly).
  • Wi-Fi Cloud Sync (Planned): Direct automatic upload from Arduino GIGA R1 WiFi to Supabase over Wi-Fi.
  • Automatic Pump Control (Planned): Relay driver circuit & safety interlock firmware (5V pumps are currently operated manually).
  • Real-World Flood Validation: Thresholds (0.50 cm / 1.25 cm) are calibrated for the miniature 3D-printed street model, not validated for full-scale NYC streets.
